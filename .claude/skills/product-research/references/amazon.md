# Amazon rules and research notes

Amazon's search ranks listings largely on relevance and on shopper behaviour (sales, conversion, availability, price, reviews). It is a different system from Google and from Etsy. Confirm current rules in Seller Central help and the category's style guide, record the date, and do not reuse Etsy tags or Shopify meta rules here.

## Eligibility and policy check comes first (can force a REJECT)
- Category approval or restrictions (gated categories, hazmat, safety and compliance documents, brand restrictions).
- Whether the seller is the seller of record. Amazon's policies restrict drop-shipping arrangements where the seller does not control the item and where another retailer's invoice or packaging reaches the buyer. Read the current policy and quote it.
- Existing listings: on Amazon you often attach to an existing product page (ASIN) and compete on the offer, rather than writing a new page. Check whether the product already has a catalogue page, and whether private label or brand registration is involved.
- Fulfilment route (FBA or merchant) and delivery time promises.

## Where research evidence comes from
- Amazon search autosuggest and result pages for target queries: sponsored versus organic placement, titles, price, review count and rating, Prime or delivery badges, "frequently bought together", "customers say" review summaries.
- Competitor product pages: bullets, A+ content, images, Q and A, review themes (read the 1 to 3 star reviews for gaps).
- Best Sellers rank and category shown on pages is VERIFIED as shown. Do not turn it into sales numbers.
- Seller Central tools (Brand Analytics, search term reports) belong to the user. Without them there is no search volume.

## Listing fields (verify per category)
- Title: limits and style rules differ by category, and Amazon enforces them. Read the category style guide. Lead with brand (if any) and product type, then key attributes (size, colour, quantity). No promotional phrases, no subjective claims, no repeated words for ranking.
- Bullet points: typically five. Each one leads with a benefit, then the supporting fact. Keep to the length guidance in the style guide.
- Description and A+ content (A+ is generally limited to brand-registered sellers, check). Use real facts and clear structure.
- Backend search terms: a hidden field with a strict byte limit (check the current limit). Do not repeat words already in the title or bullets, do not use competitor brand names, and avoid commas where spaces work. Include synonyms, spelling variants, and Spanish or other-language terms if relevant to the marketplace.
- Attributes and category-specific fields: complete as many as apply. They drive filters and relevance.
- Images: main image on a pure white background that meets the current requirements, followed by infographic, scale, detail, lifestyle, packaging and comparison images. Check the current image rules.
- Price, availability, and delivery speed affect conversion and the Buy Box.

## Keyword approach
Real shopper phrases from autosuggest and the language in competitor titles, bullets and reviews. Put the strongest buyer-intent phrase in the title and cover the rest across bullets and backend terms without repeating.

## Policy and risk checks
Prohibited claims (medical, environmental, "best seller", "free shipping" and similar in titles), trademark and brand gating, product safety compliance, review manipulation (never), and variation policy.

## Final audit additions
- Title and bullets within the category limits, no prohibited wording.
- Backend search terms within the byte limit, no repeated or competitor terms.
- Main image meets the white-background requirement.
- Claims supported, compliance documents available where the category needs them.
- Competes on the offer (price, delivery, rating) if attaching to an existing ASIN, and that was checked, not assumed.
